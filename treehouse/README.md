# Treehouse sketch

A 12 × 16 ft platform at 8 ft, built around an example white oak with a trunk about 25 in across. The porch wraps around the trunk, and a three-room cabin sits behind it. Every member is a stock Home Depot size.

- **Support:** the tree carries the porch end. A triple 2x12 header sits on two treehouse attachment bolts (TABs), with 4x4 knee braces down to a third TAB. The cabin end stands on four 6x6 posts on 12 in concrete piers, with 4x4 knee braces and 2x6 cross bracing.
- **Floor:** two doubled 2x12 beams, 2x8 joists at 16 in on center, and 2x6 decking. The joists are framed around the trunk, leaving about 2 in of clearance, and around a 32 × 23 in floor hatch.
- **Cabin:** 10 × 12 ft, with 6 ft walls of 2x4 framing covered in T1-11 siding. The gable roof is 8:12 on 2x6 rafters at 24 in, with corrugated metal panels.
  - **Main room:** 5 × 12 ft, with a table.
  - **Bunk room:** about 4 × 6 ft.
  - **Reading nook:** about 4 × 6 ft, with a window seat.
  - **Openings:** a 24 × 54 in plank door, seven acrylic windows, and a pass-through between the two back rooms.
- **Ladder:** 2x6 rails and 2x4 treads at 75°, up through the hinged hatch, with a grab handle beside it.
- **Porch railing:** 4x4 posts, 2x4 rails, 2x2 balusters spaced under 4 in, and a 2x6 cap about 37 in above the deck.

## Before building

This is a sketch, not an engineered plan. Before building:
- Have an arborist check the tree and choose the TAB locations.
- Have the platform and connections checked by an engineer or an experienced treehouse builder.
- Check local permits and frost depth.

TABs and floating brackets come from treehouse suppliers, not Home Depot. Use floating brackets at the tree, so it can sway without racking the posts. Add a gate or cover rail around the hatch opening if small children will use it.
